# Pneumonia Symptoms and Diagnostic Pathway

## Common Symptoms

Symptoms of pneumonia can range from mild to severe and often develop over one to
several days. They may include:

- Cough, which may produce phlegm (mucus)
- Fever, sweating, and chills
- Shortness of breath, especially with activity
- Rapid, shallow breathing
- Chest pain that worsens with breathing or coughing
- Fatigue and loss of appetite
- Confusion, particularly in older adults

Symptoms alone cannot confirm pneumonia — they overlap significantly with other
respiratory conditions such as bronchitis, influenza, and COVID-19.

## Diagnostic Pathway

A typical diagnostic workup after a suspicious presentation includes:

1. **Clinical evaluation** — history and physical exam, including listening to the
   lungs for crackles or abnormal breath sounds.
2. **Chest imaging** — chest X-ray is the primary imaging tool used to confirm the
   presence, location, and extent of lung infiltrates consistent with pneumonia.
3. **Laboratory testing** — complete blood count, blood cultures, and sputum culture
   may be used to help identify the causative organism, particularly in more severe
   cases.
4. **Pulse oximetry** — measures blood oxygen saturation to assess severity and the
   need for supplemental oxygen or hospitalization.
5. **Severity assessment** — clinical scoring tools help determine whether a patient
   can be safely treated as an outpatient or requires hospital admission.

## Important Caveat

A chest X-ray finding suggestive of pneumonia — including AI-assisted image analysis —
provides supportive information only. It does not replace clinical judgment, and a
qualified clinician should always confirm the diagnosis and determine appropriate
treatment.
