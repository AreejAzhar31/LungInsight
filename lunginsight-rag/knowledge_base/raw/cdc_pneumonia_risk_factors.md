# Pneumonia Risk Factors and Prevention

## Risk Factors

Certain factors increase the likelihood of developing pneumonia or experiencing more
severe illness:

- Age under 2 or over 65
- Chronic lung disease (COPD, asthma, bronchiectasis)
- Chronic conditions such as diabetes, heart failure, or liver disease
- Weakened immune system (chemotherapy, HIV, organ transplant, long-term steroid use)
- Smoking and heavy alcohol use
- Recent viral respiratory infection
- Difficulty swallowing, which increases aspiration risk
- Recent hospitalization or mechanical ventilation

## Prevention

- Vaccination — pneumococcal and annual influenza vaccines reduce risk for
  vaccine-preventable causes of pneumonia.
- Hand hygiene and respiratory etiquette to reduce transmission of respiratory
  pathogens.
- Avoiding tobacco smoke exposure.
- Managing chronic conditions that increase susceptibility.

## Important Caveat

Presence of risk factors increases pretest probability but does not confirm a
diagnosis, and their absence does not rule pneumonia out. Risk factor review is one
input among several in a full clinical assessment.
